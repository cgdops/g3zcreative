Technical SEO Services

You must be in the underlying index first. No Bing indexation, no ChatGPT citation. Technical SEO is the work of making a site crawlable, renderable, indexable, and machine-readable — which is the precondition for every ranking and every AI citation that follows.

That is why classic technical SEO is the floor, not a legacy service. G3Z Creative does this work for South Florida businesses from Miramar, and we start by telling you how many of your pages are actually in each index today.

The Index Is the Gate

An AI assistant does not read the open web when you ask it a question. It runs a retrieval step against an existing search index, pulls back a small set of candidate documents, and writes an answer out of what it pulled. Everything the model says about your market comes from that candidate set.

Which means the question that decides whether you can be cited is not “is our content good?” It is “are our pages in the index this system retrieves from?” If a URL was never crawled, was crawled but never indexed, or renders its content only after a JavaScript bundle the fetcher does not execute, it is not a candidate. It cannot be cited, quoted, summarized, or linked, no matter how well written it is.

ChatGPT's web search has been built on Bing's index, and Microsoft Copilot is Bing-native. Almost every business we audit has been watching Google Search Console for years and has never once opened Bing Webmaster Tools. That is a large, silent hole: pages that Google knows about and Bing does not, on the exact surface the AI assistants read from.

So the sequence is fixed. Crawl, then render, then index, then retrieve, then cite. You cannot skip to the end. Anyone selling you AI visibility without first proving your index coverage is selling you the fifth step of a five-step chain.

The one-line version

Indexation is a prerequisite, not a ranking factor. Ranking factors move you up a list. Indexation decides whether you are on the list at all — and the list is what the model reads.

The failure is quiet by design. Nothing errors, no alert fires, traffic just never arrives from a channel you assumed you were competing in. Most sites find out only when someone actually counts.

Once retrieval is solved, the next problem is being quotable rather than merely present — that is AI SEO and GEO, and it sits on top of this work.

The Five Steps Between Your Page and a Citation

Every step is a place a page silently drops out. Technical SEO is the discipline of finding which step yours died at.

Step 01

Crawl

A bot has to reach the URL. It fails when robots.txt blocks a directory nobody remembers adding, when a page is orphaned with no internal link pointing at it, when a faceted filter generates ten thousand near-duplicate URLs that eat the crawl budget, or when a firewall or bot-management rule quietly returns 403 to Bingbot, GPTBot, or ClaudeBot while letting Googlebot through.

What we check: robots.txt and meta directives, server logs by user agent, orphan pages, redirect chains, crawl traps, and per-bot response codes for the AI fetchers specifically.

Step 02

Render

The bot has to end up with your actual content in hand. A client-rendered app can look perfect in a browser and arrive at a crawler as an empty shell with a loading spinner. Google renders JavaScript, though on a delay; other crawlers and AI fetchers are far less generous, and several do not execute JavaScript at all.

What we check: raw HTML versus rendered DOM, whether headings, body copy, internal links, canonical tags, and JSON-LD survive without JavaScript, and whether server-side or static rendering is warranted.

Step 03

Index

Crawled is not indexed. A page can be fetched and then dropped for being a near-duplicate of another page, for carrying a canonical that points somewhere else, for a stray noindex left over from staging, or simply for being judged not worth storing. This is where the Google-versus-Bing gap shows up, and where the count that matters gets taken.

What we check: index coverage in both Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools, sitemap accuracy, canonical and duplicate resolution, and IndexNow submission so new URLs reach Bing in hours rather than weeks.

Step 04

Retrieve

Being in the index gets you into the pool. Being retrieved for a specific question means the system matched your page to that intent — which depends on unambiguous topical signals, a coherent site architecture, and structured data that states plainly what the business is, where it operates, and what it sells.

What we check: Schema.org JSON-LD coverage and validity, entity consistency across the site and your business profiles, internal linking that groups related pages, and URL and heading structure that maps to real query intent.

Step 05

Cite

The last step is the only one most “AI SEO” offers address: whether a retrieved page is written in passages a model can lift cleanly. That work is real and we do it — it is just fourth in line behind four problems that are cheaper to fix and more damaging to ignore.

Where this goes next: passage structure, direct answers under question-shaped headings, and prompt-level tracking are covered on our AI SEO page. This page is the foundation it stands on.

Running alongside

Speed & Core Web Vitals

Core Web Vitals — LCP, INP, and CLS — are a modest ranking input and a large conversion input. A slow page also gets crawled less generously, which loops back to step one. We treat performance as part of the same engineering job rather than a separate line item.

What we check: CrUX field data rather than lab scores alone, render-blocking resources, image and font delivery, third-party script weight, and layout stability on mobile.

Check Your Own Index Coverage First

Four steps, roughly twenty minutes, no agency required. If the numbers come back clean, you do not need us for this.

1. Count the URLs you believe you have

Open your XML sitemap and count the entries. That is your denominator. If you do not have a sitemap, or it lists pages that no longer exist, stop here — that is the first fix.

2. Read Google Search Console index coverage

Pages report, indexed versus not indexed, with the exclusion reason for each bucket. “Crawled — currently not indexed” and “Discovered — currently not indexed” are the two that usually hide the real problem.

3. Verify the site in Bing Webmaster Tools and read the same report

This is the step almost nobody has done. Site Explorer and the index coverage report show discovered, crawled, and indexed counts plus exclusion reasons. Compare the indexed number against your sitemap count from step one. A wide gap here is the reason AI assistants are citing your competitors.

4. Fetch one important page the way a bot does

Request the URL with JavaScript disabled, or run curl against it, and read what comes back. If your headline, body copy, internal links, and JSON-LD are not in that response, a good share of crawlers never see them either.

Technical SEO Questions, Answered

Is technical SEO still relevant with AI search?

Yes. AI answer engines do not read the open web from scratch for every question. They retrieve from an underlying search index, then summarize what they retrieved. If a page is not crawled, rendered, and indexed, it is not in the candidate pool the model draws from, so it cannot be cited. Technical SEO is the floor that makes every other visibility tactic possible.

The reason it gets called legacy work is that it stopped being novel, not that it stopped mattering. Novelty and importance are different axes. Retrieval-augmented systems made indexation more load-bearing than it was in 2015, because now a single index decides not only your rank but whether a machine writing an answer for your customer has ever seen you.

Why does Bing indexation matter for ChatGPT citations?

ChatGPT's web search has been built on Bing's search index, and Microsoft Copilot is Bing-native. If your pages are missing from Bing's index, those assistants have nothing of yours to retrieve and will cite a competitor instead. Most businesses have only ever monitored Google Search Console and have never checked their Bing index coverage.

A fair caveat: the AI vendors change their retrieval plumbing, and some run their own crawlers alongside a licensed index. The specific dependency may shift. The structural point does not — every one of these systems retrieves from some index before it writes, and Bing is currently the one with the most direct line into the assistants people actually use. Being indexed in both engines is cheap insurance against whichever way the plumbing moves next.

How do I check whether my site is indexed in Bing?

Verify the site in Bing Webmaster Tools and read the Site Explorer and index coverage reports, which show discovered, crawled, and indexed URLs plus the reason any URL was excluded. A site: query in Bing is a rough sanity check but understates coverage. Compare the indexed count against your sitemap URL count; a large gap is the problem to solve before any content or AI visibility work.

Bing Webmaster Tools will import your Google Search Console verification, so setup takes a few minutes. While you are in there, enable IndexNow — it pushes new and changed URLs to Bing directly instead of waiting to be discovered, which is the single fastest indexation win available on most sites. The four-step check above walks the whole diagnostic.

What is the difference between technical SEO and GEO?

Technical SEO makes a page retrievable: crawlable, renderable, indexable, and machine-readable. Generative engine optimization makes a retrieved page quotable: structured into direct, extractable passages an AI system will select as a source. Retrieval happens first, so technical SEO is a prerequisite for GEO rather than an alternative to it.

In practice the two share tooling and overlap heavily — schema markup, for instance, serves both. The reason to keep the distinction is diagnostic. If you are not being cited, the first question is whether you are in the pool at all. Rewriting content to be more quotable will not help a page that no crawler has stored.
